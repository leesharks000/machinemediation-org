Computational Audial Criticism (CAC) is an emerging interdisciplinary methodology that applies computational tools, digital signal processing, and machine learning to analyze the sonic, paralinguistic, and spatial properties of audio recordings. It merges Music Information Retrieval (MIR), natural language processing, and digital humanities to conduct close readings of sound.

Because sound is a continuous, multidimensional medium, CAC uses specific technical pipelines to translate acoustic data into critical insights:

Key Methodological Steps
- Spectral and Prosodic Analysis: Uses audio-to-text algorithms to capture speech cadence, pitch fluctuations, and spectral density in a recording.
- Speaker Diarization: Isolates and tracks individual voices to analyze the dynamics of who speaks, when they speak, and the interactions between subjects.
- Psycholinguistic Decoding: Analyzes the sentiment, emotion, and rhetorical intent embedded in acoustic features (like roughness or tempo) rather than just the literal words spoken.

Prominent Applications
- Media and Historical Critique: Evaluating ideological narratives, character traits, and power dynamics hidden within historical archives and propaganda films.
- Cultural Heritage Preservation: Algorithmic categorization and mapping of traditional music and spoken word to preserve endangered cultural histories.
- Digital Performance Analysis: Deconstructing and critiquing the use of audio manipulation tools like vocoders to unpack multiplied identities and witness-textures in audio art.
