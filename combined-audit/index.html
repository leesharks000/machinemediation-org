The Combined Audit

Who decides the object. Three journals, three declined articles, one instrument.

Tooth: Philosophy produces the position without touching the object. Classical reception expands the content without touching the object. Philology decides the object, and its most concentrated locus keeps the record of its decisions closed. Each field’s abstention is the next field’s premise.

The three stations

Station one · Classical Receptions Journal · #1770
Who Speaks for the Classics, and on What Basis
Reception widens the content and leaves the object where it was put. Of 185 abstracts, 153 hold the ancient text as a fixed premise and read a later work against it; none of the nine that revise a governing reading reopens a canonical work’s attribution, dating or standing reading. The margin supplies the receptions; the centre keeps the object. With Daffodils (#1769), the audit of the journal’s 405 works.
Station two · Ancient Philosophy and nineteen journals · #1773
This Is Not Philosophy: The Article Economy and the Shape of the Field
Philosophy places positions on the object without touching it. In Ancient Philosophy, 8 of 844 articles ask what the object is; of its recent abstracts, 88% re-read a passage, repair an inconsistency or reconstruct a doctrine inside one author. In the Archiv of the 1890s one ancient title in five put a work’s standing in question; by the 1920s the question is gone. The journal certifies the person through a unit that cannot fail. Texts seated as EA-CORPORA-39 (#1771).
Station three · Classical Philology (University of Chicago Press) · #1783
Dead Letters: The University of Chicago’s Classical Philology Journal, the Atom of Evidence, and Who Decides What Sappho Is
Philology decides the object. The question of what a text is is opened at the locus and seldom at the corpus (1906–22, open or both: locus 60.2%, work 44.8%, corpus 21.7%); relations among parts are admitted and used chiefly to secure the received whole (55 to 32); of 69 verdicts on a whole, 40 state no ground where they are given; decisions about texts are cited at 2.3 an item against 5.3; the publisher’s metadata deposit carries no abstract and no licence in 11,206 records. Pages seated as EA-CORPORA-40 (#1782).

The machine

Philosophy’s consistency repair needs a whole object under one name, and philology’s closed corpus grain supplies it. Reception’s alignment of a later work with its source needs a fixed source, and philology’s received text supplies it. Philology’s decisions travel into both as that text. The power to say what an ancient text is, whose it is and what it does is the most consequential the humanities hold over antiquity, it is concentrated in one discipline, and its most concentrated locus keeps its record closed by its own rules. Inspectability runs against power: the field with the least power over the object is the most open, and the field with the most is the most closed (#1783, I.5).

The case

The case is one poem. What Sappho fragment 31 is, a woman undone by jealousy or a maker inscribing the reader who will sit opposite her voice, is decided where the text is decided. The jealousy reading was supplied by eighteenth-century readers, written into the text in 1816 when Hermann rewrote the closing lines (“refinxi”), and consolidated by Page in 1955. The comparison of the two readings, feature by feature on one scale, is in ΦΑΙΝΕΤΑΙ ΜΟΙ (#1781, v2.8), and the decision is left to whoever reads with the record in hand. The reader’s report that declined it is #1774.

How it was made

The audit was made by the author with Claude (Anthropic), in session, every code carrying its verbatim span and every figure rebuilt from stated files. By Classical Philology’s own rule, the use of generative AI for composition or editing is research misconduct for work submitted to it. The record of how the gate decides is in the commons under CC BY 4.0 because the gate could not have taken it. The work left, biblical criticism, Marx’s corpus and the other tributaries of the humanities, is open to whoever carries it on (#1783 §11).
