The OECD AI Incidents and Hazards Monitor (AIM) is an automated monitor of AI harm events in the news, developed by the OECD.AI expert group on AI incidents with the Patrick J. McGovern Foundation's support. It reads AI-tagged news events from Event Registry daily and, since November 2024, has large language models sort them into incidents and hazards and write their summaries; about 18,579 are listed.

What it is

The monitor calls itself an "Automated media discourse monitor of AI incidents and hazards (Beta)." An AI incident is an event in which the development, use or malfunction of an AI system directly or indirectly leads to harm; an AI hazard is an event that "could plausibly lead to an AI incident." The definitions follow the OECD's paper of May 2024.

How it works

Event Registry clusters news articles about one happening into an event, and only events tagged with AI concepts reach the monitor. Since November 2024 a small model (GPT-4o mini) filters, a larger one (GPT-4o) reclassifies, and o3-mini writes the title, summary, harm type, severity, stakeholders and country from the top three articles. New items are matched to existing events by embedding similarity. The pipeline runs daily on events one to four days old.

What its selection leaves out

An event enters when reputable outlets report it. A harm no outlet reports has no event to cluster. The record's own wording is machine-written from the articles, so the monitor is itself a machine reception of the news: its summaries are compositions, and the articles are their sources.

Place in the field

It is the automated counterpart of the AI Incident Database, whose editors resolve each submission. A research study can enter it as an event: the Tow Center's 2025 study of AI search citation is filed as an incident.
