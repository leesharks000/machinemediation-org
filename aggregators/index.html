MMRS meta-aggregator

The aggregators of machine-mediated reception work, each formalized as an entity: what it is in its own words, who runs it, what it admits and in what unit, what it leaves out, and where its record goes when it ends. 20 aggregators, compiled 2026-10-29.
Academ-AI
databasemonitoringactive
Academ-AI is Alex Glynn's database of published scholarship with suspected undeclared use of AI, identified by chatbot phrasing and quoted with the phrases highlighted: 772 examples in August 2025 (633 journal articles, 111 conference papers, 28 books and chapters).
AEO Vision — top arXiv papers for GEO
curated listoptimizationactive
AEO Vision's list of the top arXiv papers for generative engine optimization, dated 15 July 2026 and bylined to its content team, summarizes 12 papers, each with a takeaway for teams.
AI Hallucination Cases
databasemonitoringreceptionactive
AI Hallucination Cases is Damien Charlotin's database of legal decisions in which a court or tribunal finds or implies reliance on hallucinated material: 2,231 decisions worldwide as of 28 October 2026, CC BY 4.0, with a CSV export.
AI Incident Database
databaseharmmonitoringactive
The AI Incident Database (AIID) is an editor-resolved database of AI harm events, run by the Responsible AI Collaborative since 2020. Its unit is the incident, with the reports that document it attached, and every incident has a stable citation page at incidentdatabase.ai/cite/N.
AI Overview Capture Registry
databasereceptionprovenanceactive
The AI Overview Capture Registry, run by the Crimson Hexagonal Archive, records dated compositions by machine answer surfaces at named addresses, each with its verbatim transcript and the sources it engaged: 792 captures and 981 observations at registry version 12.345.
AIAAIC Repository
databaseharmactive
The AIAAIC Repository records incidents and controversies driven by AI, algorithmic and automation systems. It began in June 2019 as a private, professional project, is licensed CC BY-SA 4.0, and is the subject of the AIAAIC Alert newsletter written by Charlie Pownall.
Awesome-GEO (DavidHuji)
curated listoptimizationactive
Awesome-GEO is a GitHub awesome list of research on generative engine optimization maintained by DavidHuji: about 27 papers, code, datasets and tools in sections by year (2024–2026) and open-source resources, with 123 stars.
CMS AI and Copyright Case Tracker
databaseprovenanceactive
The CMS AI and Copyright Case Tracker, compiled by the law firm CMS, summarizes 23 court cases on AI and copyright from 10 jurisdictions, led by Germany; the newest entry is dated 31 July 2026, and no US cases are listed.
CodeSOTA
databasediscoveryactive
CodeSOTA, by Kacper Wikiel for Fabryka AI in Warsaw, is a routing layer for AI tasks on its homepage ("One Routing Layer for AI Tasks") and a successor to Papers with Code on its /papers-with-code/ pages ("Codesota is the v2 replacement for what PWC did"). It offers leaderboards with verification dates, an open JSON registry and /api/sota.
Data Provenance Explorer
databaseprovenancemonitoringactive
The Data Provenance Explorer, from the Data Provenance Initiative (Longpre, Mahari and others), traces the sources and licences of AI training datasets: nearly 4,000 text, speech and video datasets by its own count. Its founding audit covered more than 1,800 text datasets and found licence omission above 70% and error rates above 50%.
MIT AI Risk Repository
databaseharmactive
The MIT AI Risk Repository is a database of more than 1,700 AI risks extracted from 65 published risk frameworks, run by the MIT AI Risk Initiative (MIT FutureTech). Version 4 went live in December 2025; its AI Incident Tracker classifies AI Incident Database reports against the same taxonomy.
NewsGuard AI Tracking Center
monitormonitoringactive
NewsGuard's AI Tracking Center catalogues AI content farm websites, unreliable news sites run with little or no human oversight: 3,749 in 16 languages as of 23 June 2026. It also hosts NewsGuard's recurring AI False Claim Monitor.
OECD AI Incidents and Hazards Monitor
monitorharmmonitoringactive
The OECD AI Incidents and Hazards Monitor (AIM) is an automated monitor of AI harm events in the news, run by OECD.AI. It reads AI-tagged news events from Event Registry daily and, since November 2024, has large language models sort them into incidents and hazards and write their summaries; about 18,579 are listed.
Papers with Code
databasediscoveryended
Papers with Code (2018–2025) linked machine learning papers to their code and reported results, and its benchmark tables were the field's map of the state of the art. Founded by Robert Stojnic and Ross Taylor in 2018 and majority-owned by Facebook (later Meta) from December 2019, paperswithcode.com was sunset on 24 July 2025, and its data survives in CC BY-SA dumps. Its name now has a live instance, a revival at paperswithcode.co.
Papers with Code (paperswithcode.co)
databasediscoveryactive
Papers with Code at paperswithcode.co is a revival of the dead aggregator, built by Niels Rogge of Hugging Face's open-source team and announced in May 2026; its code, in Hugging Face's GitHub organization under Apache-2.0, "recreates the original paperswithcode.com, which Meta retired in 2025."
Presenc AI — GEO academic papers 2026
curated listoptimizationactive
Presenc AI's GEO academic papers page, published 23 April 2026 by its CTO, gathers 34 papers and reports on generative engine optimization in five themes, each with a vendor note on what it means for practitioners.
Problematic Paper Screener
monitormonitoringactive
The Problematic Paper Screener, run by Guillaume Cabanac with Cyril Labbé and Alexander Magazinov since 27 February 2021, scans the scientific literature weekly for papers generated or disguised by tools (tortured phrases, SCIgen, Mathgen) and for references to retracted work.
Search Arena
leaderboardevaluationactive
Search Arena ranks search-enabled language models by head-to-head human votes: 247,944 votes across 22 models at its update of 25 February 2026. Its dataset paper (ICLR 2026) found that users prefer answers with more citations even when the citations do not support the claims.
