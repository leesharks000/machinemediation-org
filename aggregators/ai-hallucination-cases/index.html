What it is

Its unit is the decision. A case enters when a court or tribunal addresses AI use in more than a passing reference and explicitly finds, or implies, reliance on hallucinated material. Mere allegations are excluded, with some judgment calls admitted. Each record carries the court, date, the party that used AI, the tool where named, the nature of the hallucination (fabricated, false quotes, misrepresented, outdated advice), and the outcome and sanction.

What it shows

Of its cases, 1,288 involve pro se litigants and 880 lawyers. The period filter begins in the second quarter of 2023. The database grows by its editor's reading and by cases readers send.

What its selection leaves out

It states its own boundary: it does not track "the (necessarily wider) universe of all fake citations or use of AI in court filings." A fabricated citation enters when a court catches and rules on it.

Place in the field

It is the census's most exact record of citation fidelity. Each entry is a machine composition that reached a court with sources it invented or misrepresented, and the court's decision is the reception that recorded it.
