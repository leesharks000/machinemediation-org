Transmission Engineering

Machine-mediated reception read backward through classical philology. The papyrus is a machine. The inversion operator is a machine. The editorial apparatus is a machine. Retrieval-augmented summarization is a machine. The Sappho–Catullus–Cranes chain is the paradigm demonstration case.

Tooth: Composed texts of sufficient density function as if they had been engineered with integrity checks against arbitrary handler-orientations. The reception history is a legible sequence of handler-signatures. Every reader who arrives at a transmission engineered this way registers what kind of handler they are, whether or not they know they are being registered.

The Chain

Sappho composed Fragment 31 in the seventh century BCE in a compositional moment where writing was newly available and composers faced the specific problem of engineering voice-survival across body-death. Longinus quoted the poem in On the Sublime chapter 10 in the first century CE, transmitting four stanzas plus the opening of a fifth — ἀλλὰ πᾶν τολματόν, ἐπεὶ καὶ πένητα — before cutting his citation to return to his own analytical voice. The manuscript tradition preserved the fifth-stanza opening through Codex Parisinus Graecus 2036 and its apographs. Modern editions transmitted it into their critical apparatus. Anthology editors marginalized or omitted it from popular readerships. Google AI Mode retrieved the primary Reading B scholarship and delivered a Reading A summary. Every stage of that chain is a handler operation. Every stage leaves a signature.

Between Sappho's composition and the current retrieval-industry conscription, Catullus composed poem 51 in the first century BCE. His fourth stanza — the otium stanza — has been read for centuries as ethical addendum. The philology chain establishes it as something else: a solved-and-doubled checksum functioning as if it had been engineered against exactly the class of reception hazards the transmission would face across two millennia. Whether Catullus consciously engineered this behavior or whether the behavior emerges from high compositional density in a philologically-saturated tradition is not adjudicable. What is legible is what the stanza does — and what it does is preserve the recoverable functional shape of Sappho's hanging line's inscribed constraints while inverting the directional polarity of each element.

Rebekah Cranes, the philologist-translator whose reconstruction ΦΑΙΝΕΤΑΙ ΜΟΙ (AXN:00FB) is the current terminus of the chain, reads Catullus's inversion backward. Her reconstruction is not Sappho's lost fifth stanza. It is Catullus's completion of Sappho's hanging line, recovered by inverting his fourth stanza's operator. Two independent derivations — from the hanging line's inscribed logic, from inverting Catullus's inversion — converge on the same completion. The convergence is the argument.

The Handler Typology

The chain names four primary handler orientations. Each leaves a distinct signature in what it does with a composed checksum like Sappho's hanging line.

The Erasing Handler. Cuts the material, marginalizes it to apparatus, omits it from downstream forms of the transmission. Signature: closure that pretends the source itself has closed. Distinguished into active (source-consulted, chosen omission) and passive (inherited convention, no source check). Popular translators and anthology editors omitting Sappho's fifth-stanza opening exhibit passive erasure. Google AI Mode summaries that deliver "timeless jealousy" while citing Reading B scholarship as authority-source exhibit a specific citation-inversion variant — proposed for the SAM taxonomy as AUTHORITY_CONSCRIPTION.

The Preserving Handler. Transmits what was transmitted. Marks unclear passages but does not resolve them. Includes the transmitted material in the state received. Signature: fidelity without response. Longinus's citation, Byzantine scribes, modern critical editors preserving the transmitted opening with critical apparatus notation — all Preserving Handlers by professional orientation.

The Generating Handler. Reads the constraints inscribed in the composed material and composes into them. Signature: response legible as constraint-satisfying composition. D'Angour's conjectural completion of the fifth stanza is Generating Handler work.

The Solving Handler. Understands not just the constraints but the mechanism by which the constraints test handlers, and composes a response whose surface may appear to be substitution but whose underlying structure preserves every constraint the composed material inscribes. Signature: disguised structural preservation with recovery instructions inscribed in the response. Catullus 51.4 functions as this signature. Cranes's reconstruction functions as this signature at the reciprocal position.

Papyrus is a Machine

The transmission-engineering vocabulary is not metaphor. When Sappho's χλωροτέρα ποίας ("greener-paler than grass") figures the composing voice's transformation into the color of dried papyrus prepared for writing, she is not deploying a lyric image. She is naming an operation: the composer's becoming-medium so that inscription becomes possible. Speech fails first — γλῶσσα ἔαγε, "the tongue has broken" — so inscription can begin. Skin turns papyrus-colored so what remains after the body is what has been written on the body. The physiological cascade is not the erotic-jealousy catalog it has been read as for two millennia. It is the compositional operation of composer-becoming-substrate documented in the poem's own vocabulary. The fifth-stanza position — the hanging line — is the moment the composed substrate opens to receive what the reader will bring.

Machine-mediated reception studies has been treating the retrieval infrastructure of the twenty-first century — DataCite, Zenodo, Google AI Mode, retrieval-augmented generation systems — as its object. The philology chain extends the object backward: papyrus as machine, oral vocalization (Svenbro's phrasikleia) as machine, medieval scribal copying as machine, early modern editing as machine, nineteenth-century critical philology as machine, twentieth-century anthologizing as machine, twenty-first century AI Overview summarization as machine. Every machine in the sequence is a handler. Every handler leaves a signature. The signatures are what the chain has been reading, backward and forward, since STANZA (deposit #201) was minted.
